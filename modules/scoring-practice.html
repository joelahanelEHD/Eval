Before you begin: how the scale works
UnacceptableRarely / Never. Consistently below the standard.
DevelopingInconsistently. Needs more support than a typical first-year teacher.
ProficientConsistently. Needs only the support a first-year teacher gets. This is the program target.
ExemplaryModel for colleagues. Considerably surpasses a first-year teacher.
  • Rate the evidence against the descriptor, not against the calendar. Developing is expected at midterm, but a midterm candidate who meets the Proficient descriptor earns Proficient, and a final-evaluation candidate who doesn't, doesn't.
  • Look for the words that separate levels. On most items the jump from Developing to Proficient is about consistency, and the jump to Exemplary adds something new, often students taking ownership.
  • Exemplary is a high bar. Ask: would I send a colleague to watch this candidate do it? Candidates are not expected to be Exemplary in most areas.
  • Watch for "equitably." Several descriptors require that the practice reaches all students. Uneven treatment can hold a rating down even when the strategy is good.
  • Use "No Opportunity to Observe" only when you truly saw nothing related to the item.

Why this matters: in 2026, mentors and clinical coaches were usually within one level of each other, but exact agreement was close to chance on items like Instructional Dialogue and Expectations for Student Behavior. Mentors also rated higher than coaches on average, most of all in Instructional Delivery.
